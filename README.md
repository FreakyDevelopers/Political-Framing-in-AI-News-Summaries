# Political Framing and Accuracy in AI-Generated News Summaries: A Small-Scale Comparative Study

## Overview

This repository contains an independent student research project examining how free and publicly accessible AI chatbots summarize politically sensitive news articles.

The study compares summaries generated by five AI systems: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, and DeepSeek. Each model was given the same neutral summarization prompt and the same set of six immigration-related news articles. The resulting thirty summaries were manually coded for neutrality, emotional language, political framing, accuracy, and completeness.

The full research paper is available in the `/paper` folder, and the dataset and coding workbook are available in the `/data` folder.

## Research Questions

1. Do free or publicly accessible AI models differ in neutrality, accuracy, completeness, emotional language, and political framing when summarizing news?
2. Does the source orientation of a news article affect how AI summaries frame the same broad topic area?
3. Which models performed most consistently in this small dataset?

## Methodology Summary

The project used a small comparative research design:

- Six politically sensitive news articles were selected.
- Articles were grouped by source orientation: left, center, and right.
- Five publicly accessible AI models summarized each article using the same neutral prompt.
- The study collected thirty AI-generated summaries.
- Each summary was manually coded using a consistent rubric.
- Model performance was compared across neutrality, emotional language, political framing, accuracy, and completeness.

The final quality score in the workbook is a simple average of neutrality, accuracy, and completeness. Emotional language and political framing were analyzed separately because they measure tone and orientation rather than overall quality.

## Key Findings

This study found that all five AI models were capable of producing readable summaries, but they did not perform identically.

Key findings from the research paper include:

- Claude had the highest average quality score in the dataset.
- Grok performed strongly on completeness and often preserved more article-specific detail.
- ChatGPT often produced smooth and balanced prose but sometimes omitted important details.
- Gemini and DeepSeek showed more variation depending on the source article and framing.
- Center-source articles produced the cleanest cross-model results.
- Left-source articles tended to produce more negative political framing scores.
- Right-source articles tended to produce more positive political framing scores.
- The results suggest that AI summaries can sound neutral while still shaping how readers understand news through selection, emphasis, omission, and inherited source framing.

## Limitations

This project is an exploratory pilot study, not a final general theory of AI bias.

Main limitations include:

- The dataset is small: six articles and thirty summaries.
- The study focuses on one topic area: immigration-related news.
- The summaries were manually coded by one researcher.
- The project did not use multiple coders or inter-coder reliability testing.
- The study used free or publicly accessible model versions rather than paid enterprise APIs.
- AI systems change over time, so the results should be read as a snapshot of public-facing model behavior during the collection period.
